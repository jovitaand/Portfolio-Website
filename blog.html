PERSONAL ESSAY · JOVITA ANDREWS

“My Journey to a Master’s in the United States: Finding My Path in Applied Data Science”

Pursuing a Master’s in the United States was far from straightforward, despite how it might look on paper. Transitioning from a background in Computer Science and Engineering into the world of Applied Data Science involved much more than simply following a logical path; it required a journey of self-discovery, a bit of soul-searching, and a lot of determination. Right after my undergraduate studies, I embarked on what I call my “eat, pray, and love” journey, hoping to rediscover myself, find a fulfilling career path, and—just as importantly—choose one that would be sustainable and rewarding in the long term. I spent countless hours weighing various options, from blockchain technology to cybersecurity and eventually to computer science with a focus on data science.

The process of searching for the right program that would align with my ambitions was both exciting and daunting. I wanted to find a program that not only challenged me intellectually but also fit my vision of a future where I could build a fulfilling career—and perhaps even start a family one day. It was clear to me from the start that I wasn’t just looking for any program; I was looking for one that would ignite my passion, not just sustain it. I wanted a degree that would prepare me for a cutting-edge field, and I needed to feel that every class I took was directly relevant to my future career.

After many long nights of research, I accidentally stumbled upon Syracuse University while exploring schools in New York. The idea of studying in New York had always intrigued me—a bit of a “Carrie Bradshaw moment” calling me to experience life in this vibrant part of the world. My first application was to the School of Engineering and Computer Science (ECS) – Master of Science in Computer Science program. However, I quickly realized that this wasn’t the right fit for my goals and interests. That’s when I discovered the School of Information Studies (iSchool) – Master of Science in Applied Data Science program.

As I delved into the details of the program, I was immediately struck by how well-defined and intentional the curriculum was. The iSchool laid out a clear, structured path to a data science career, which stood out after seeing so many vague program descriptions during my research. Each required course was directly relevant to the current job market, and the curriculum offered real-world applications rather than theoretical fluff. What particularly drew me to the iSchool was its unique program structure, which was designed to cater to students’ varied career aspirations. Courses were organized into specialized tracks, like Artificial Intelligence, Big Data Analytics, Data and Business Analytics, Data Pipelines and Platforms, Language Analytics, Project Management, and Visual Analytics. This focus on career-aligned pathways set Syracuse apart from other universities. I knew right then that this program would not only meet my aspirations but also give me a solid footing in today’s competitive job market.

As I reflect on my journey through the program, I realize just how much I gained from the iSchool’s emphasis on practical, job-ready skills. The learning outcomes were designed to ensure that we could master the full data science life cycle and make an impact in diverse fields. The outcomes that defined my journey at the iSchool can be broken down as follows: collecting, storing, and accessing data through the use of relevant technologies; generating actionable insights across various societal, business, and political contexts; applying visualization and predictive models to drive meaningful insights; using programming languages like R and Python to support analytical processes; communicating findings effectively to a range of audiences; and practicing ethical data science by addressing fairness, bias, transparency, and privacy in our analyses.

These learning outcomes provided a roadmap through my courses, each of which helped me build foundational skills in data science. Data collection and storage were areas I honed in on through courses like IST 659: Data Administration Concepts and Database Management, where I learned database management, SQL and Microsoft Azure. This skill became essential in projects like the Text-to-Speech Converter, where I used cloud technologies to store and manage data efficiently. By understanding the intricacies of data storage, I was able to handle large-scale data applications with ease, laying the groundwork for future projects that required a deep understanding of cloud-based data solutions.

Generating actionable insights across various contexts was another major outcome I achieved during the program. Courses like SCM 651: Business Analytics and IST 686: Quantitative Reasoning for Data Science were instrumental in teaching me how to analyze data from diverse sources to uncover meaningful patterns and make strategic decisions by using the power of Microsoft Excel, R and Statistics for Data Science. For instance, in my NBA Analytics project, I used statistical reasoning to analyze player performance metrics, predicting outcomes like MVP awards based on player data. This project taught me how to draw valuable insights that could inform decision-making in professional settings, particularly in business and sports analytics.

Applying visualization and predictive models to generate insights became a major skill I refined through hands-on projects. In IST 707: Applied Machine Learning, I developed my modeling skills through projects like Crime Classification Based on Geographical Location, where I used clustering techniques to identify crime hotspots across Los Angeles neighborhoods. Visualizing crime patterns provided actionable insights for public safety agencies, showing me the power of data visualization in conveying complex information to a broad audience. In this course I learned how to leverage machine learning packages such as matplotlib, sci-kit learn, numpy, NLTK, seaborn, pandas, ggplot2 and dplyr through languages Python as well as R.

Programming skills in R and Python were key aspects of my education at the iSchool. These languages formed the backbone of every project, from building regression models to developing deep learning models. In the Movie Reviews Sentiment Analysis project, I used advanced NLP techniques and tools like TensorFlow and BERT to classify reviews by sentiment. This project was an incredible learning experience, not only for understanding sentiment analysis but also for seeing the power of Python in managing data-heavy tasks, processing text, and refining deep learning models to produce meaningful results.

One of the most valuable skills I developed was the ability to communicate insights effectively to different audiences. In IST 718: Big Data Analytics, I learned to translate complex data findings into accessible language for both technical and non-technical stakeholders. I applied this skill in projects like the Taxi Fare Predictor, where I presented insights on fare dynamics in a user-friendly format, making complex data understandable to non-experts. This ability to communicate clearly and effectively has become an invaluable skill, one I know will be crucial as I move into my career.

Finally, the program emphasized the importance of ethical considerations in data science. In IST 692: Responsible AI, I learned about bias mitigation, transparency, and fairness in AI. Applying these principles to projects like the Movie Reviews Sentiment Analysis helped me recognize the impact of biases in data and models, prompting me to take steps to minimize unintended biases in my work. This ethical focus has made me a more thoughtful and responsible data scientist, understanding the broader impact of data science on society.

Reflecting on this journey, I feel a deep appreciation for the program’s rigor, the faculty’s guidance, and the invaluable hands-on experience I gained through each course. One of my favorite classes was IST 707: Applied Machine Learning, where I got to build machine learning models from scratch. This class challenged me to push my understanding of machine learning concepts and equipped me with a solid foundation that I carried into subsequent projects. The best part of the program was its emphasis on ethics, especially in the Responsible AI course. It came as a surprise just how much thought goes into ensuring fair and unbiased models, a reminder that data science is about more than just numbers—it’s about responsibility and making a positive impact.

My journey at Syracuse University also involved hands-on roles that significantly enhanced my academic experience. My position as a Teaching Assistant for the invent@SU program gave me a unique opportunity to work with undergraduate students, guiding them through ideation, design, prototyping, and pitching their inventions. Working under the guidance of Professor Alexander K. Deyhim, I supported students as they transformed innovative ideas into prototypes and pitched their products to evaluators. My responsibilities included creating and updating websites for the invent@SU, Mechanical Engineering Capstone Project, and Entrepreneurship programs. Additionally, I managed event photography, social media marketing, inventory control, project budgeting, and stipends. This experience taught me the importance of clear communication, project management, and creative problem-solving, which are invaluable skills in data science. The hybrid nature of this role also taught me to adapt and balance both in-person and remote tasks efficiently.

During my time as a Research Assistant at the Syracuse University School of Information Studies, I gained hands-on experience with social media analysis under Professor Yiqi Li. This role involved collecting and analyzing data on interactions among various ethnic groups, including Asian Americans, Pacific Islanders, and Native Hawaiians, on Twitter. I conducted web scraping to gather datasets, focusing on conversation patterns and behavior among diverse users associated with organizations like the Asian American Psychological Association (AAPA) and the Japanese American Citizens League (JACL). This project honed my skills in data scraping, social media analysis, and understanding cultural dynamics, emphasizing the social relevance of data science. The opportunity to work with real-world data, exploring cultural interactions, showed me how data science could contribute to social understanding and even social change.

Working as a Data Analyst and Communication Intern at SyracuseCoE (NYS’s Center of Excellence in Environmental and Energy Systems) provided another layer of experience in applying data science to real-world challenges. My primary responsibilities involved maintaining and enhancing databases of partners, stakeholders, and ongoing projects, ensuring data accuracy and accessibility. I conducted targeted research to identify grant opportunities for our partners and supported weekly strategic outreach initiatives. Additionally, I managed event logistics, created engaging content for social media, and assisted in broader marketing and outreach efforts to promote SyracuseCoE’s mission. Working under the mentorship of Tamara L. Rosanio, I learned invaluable lessons in stakeholder engagement, database management, and content creation, which have become core skills in my data science toolkit. Through this role, I learned the importance of communication in data science, as I regularly crafted stories to showcase impactful SyracuseCoE projects and collaborations, making technical information accessible and engaging for a broad audience.

Throughout my journey in the Master’s in Applied Data Science program, my favorite class was undoubtedly Business Analytics, taught by Professor Donald Harter. His quick wit and passion for the subject kept me thoroughly engaged for the entire three-hour sessions. Professor Harter’s enthusiasm and deep knowledge made a significant impact, laying a strong foundation in analytics that prepared me for the more advanced courses I would tackle in the semesters that followed.

One of the best parts of the program was the collaboration with the Whitman School of Management, allowing me to take courses that bridged data science and business. I appreciated the challenge of each Whitman course, which pushed me to develop a well-rounded skill set and apply data science in business contexts. However, I encountered an unexpected hurdle along the way: I had to petition to enroll in a few courses that were initially restricted for iSchool students. While I was ultimately able to take these classes, I found this limitation frustrating, as I believe that academic programs should be flexible, allowing students to fully customize their learning paths based on their interests and goals. This experience emphasized the importance of academic flexibility in fostering a richer, more tailored educational journey.

The program provided me with a well-rounded, comprehensive skill set that I know will serve me well as I enter the job market. The iSchool’s Master of Science in Applied Data Science program has prepared me to tackle data science challenges with confidence, practical knowledge, and ethical integrity. Looking back on the long hours of research and the countless applications I reviewed; I am glad I chose Syracuse University. This program not only met my aspirations but exceeded them, fueling my passion for data science and setting me on a path where I feel equipped to make a meaningful difference. My journey was challenging, but it has brought me to a place where I am ready to enter the data-driven world with the skills, insights, and ethical awareness I gained through iSchool.
